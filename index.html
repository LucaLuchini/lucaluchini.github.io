Compact PCB Test & Programming Fixture

Designed and built a custom fixture for accurately locating a small production PCB and reliably contacting densely spaced test points during programming and electrical testing.

Role Mechanical design, CAM, machining, fabrication, assembly
Tools SolidWorks, Fusion 360, CNC router, FDM printing
Hardware Delrin insert, pogo pins, toggle clamps, 3D-printed components, purchased hardware

01

The Challenge

The fixture needed to repeatedly position a very small PCB while establishing reliable electrical contact with a dense group of test points.

Because the test points were closely spaced, even small positional errors could cause pogo pins to miss their intended locations or make inconsistent contact.

The design also needed to be practical to manufacture, assemble, service, and hand off to the test group for routine use.

Primary design challenge: maintain repeatable PCB location and pogo-pin alignment while keeping the fixture simple, manufacturable, and serviceable.

02

Fixture Design

I designed the fixture in SolidWorks using the supplied PCB geometry and test-point locations as the basis for the mechanical interface.

Off-the-shelf components were used where practical, including toggle clamps and standard hardware, while the board interface, fixture body, spring-supported structure, and locating components were custom designed.

This approach reduced unnecessary custom fabrication while still allowing the critical geometry around the PCB to be controlled.

03

Precision Delrin Insert

Because the PCB test points were densely spaced, positional accuracy at the pogo-pin interface was critical.

Rather than relying on FDM tolerances for this region, I designed a separate Delrin insert and CNC-machined the pogo-pin pattern.

This allowed the bulk of the fixture to be manufactured quickly using 3D printing while reserving CNC machining for the area where tighter positional control was required.

I generated the CAM in Fusion 360 and machined the insert on a CNC router before integrating it into the final assembly.

04

Assembly & Wiring

After fabricating the mechanical components, I installed the pogo-pin hardware and assembled the fixture.

I soldered the wiring into the pogo-pin holders and routed the connections so the completed fixture could be integrated with the electrical test and programming setup.

The completed fixture was then transferred to the test group for programming and electrical verification.

05

Outcome

The final fixture combined FDM-printed structural components, purchased hardware, toggle clamps, pogo pins, and a precision machined Delrin interface into a complete test and programming fixture.

The project required mechanical design, tolerance awareness, component selection, additive manufacturing, CNC machining, soldering, assembly, and coordination with the downstream test team.
