Compact PCB Test & Programming Fixture

Designed and built a compact fixture for reliably locating a small PCB and contacting tightly spaced test points during programming and electrical testing.

Role Mechanical design, CAM, machining, assembly
Tools SolidWorks, Fusion 360, CNC router, FDM printing
Hardware 3D-printed components, Delrin insert, pogo pins, off-the-shelf hardware
hero.jpg — final fixture

01

The Challenge

The fixture needed to hold a very small PCB in a repeatable position while making reliable electrical contact with closely spaced test points. The tight geometry made a conventional fully 3D-printed locating feature impractical for the critical contact area.

The design also needed to be practical to assemble, service, and hand off to the test team for regular use.

Primary design problem: achieve repeatable board location and pogo-pin alignment at a scale where small positional errors could prevent reliable contact.

02

Fixture Design

I designed the fixture around the existing PCB geometry and test-point locations. Off-the-shelf hardware was incorporated where possible, while custom components were created for board location, support, and pogo-pin positioning.

03

Precision Delrin Insert

Because the board and test points were small and closely spaced, I used a machined Delrin insert for the critical locating region. This provided a more controlled geometry than relying on the 3D-printed fixture body alone.

I created the CAM in Fusion 360 and machined the insert on a CNC router, then integrated it into the printed fixture assembly.

04

Assembly & Wiring

After fabricating the mechanical components, I assembled the fixture and soldered the wiring into the pogo-pin holders. The completed fixture was then transferred to the test team for electrical testing and programming work.

05

Outcome

The finished fixture combined rapid 3D-printed fabrication with a precision machined interface where tighter control was required. The result was a complete test/programming fixture ready for use by the test group.

This project required mechanical design, tolerance awareness, selection and integration of purchased components, additive manufacturing, CNC machining, soldering, and communication with the downstream test team.
